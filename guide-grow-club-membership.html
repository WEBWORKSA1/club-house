Growth

How to Grow Club Membership: 21 Proven Tactics

By Club.House Editorial · Updated October 30, 2026 · Related guides

Practical ways to recruit new members, improve retention and build referral loops for any club.

Most clubs don't fail because nobody is interested. They fail because new people can't find them, don't feel welcome on day one, or quietly drift away by month three. Growth is therefore three separate problems — discovery, conversion and retention — and each needs its own tactics.

Discovery: get found by the right people

  1. Be listed everywhere your members search. Your club should appear on Club.House, the major interest platforms for your niche, local community boards and your library's events page.
  2. Use a specific name and description. "Beginner-friendly Saturday trail runs, 5–8 km, all paces" converts far better than "Running Club".
  3. Post photos of real meetings. Faces and places signal that a group is active and friendly.
  4. Partner with a related business. Bookshops, running stores, game cafés and co-working spaces often promote clubs that bring them foot traffic.
  5. Run one public "open night" per quarter designed for first-timers, with a short intro and low commitment.
  6. Ask local media and newsletters for a community-calendar listing. Most are free.
  7. Show up where your future members already are — races, conventions, school fairs, farmers' markets.

Conversion: turn visitors into members

  1. Reply to every inquiry within 48 hours. Speed is the strongest conversion lever you control.
  2. Assign a greeter at every meeting whose only job is welcoming new faces.
  3. Explain how things work in the first five minutes — newcomers hate guessing.
  4. Offer a free first visit and make paying dues easy afterwards.
  5. Follow up the next day with a short personal message and the next date.
  6. Make the second visit the goal, not the sign-up. People who come twice usually stay.

Retention: keep people coming back

  1. Keep a fixed rhythm. Predictable dates are easier to protect in busy calendars.
  2. Give members small jobs — bringing snacks, choosing a topic, hosting once. Contribution creates belonging.
  3. Celebrate milestones: anniversaries, personal bests, 10th meetings.
  4. Create sub-groups once you pass about 25 active members so everyone still feels known.
  5. Ask for feedback twice a year with a three-question survey.
  6. Notice absences. A friendly "missed you" message brings back a surprising number of drifting members.

Referral loops: let members recruit

  1. "Bring a friend" nights make inviting socially easy.
  2. Give members something shareable — a photo, a recap, a results list — after every meeting.

What to measure

Track four numbers monthly: inquiries, first-time attendees, second-time attendees, and active members. If inquiries are low, work on discovery. If first-timers don't return, fix your welcome. If active members fall, look at rhythm and engagement.
