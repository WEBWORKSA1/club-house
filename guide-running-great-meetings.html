Meetings

How to Run Club Meetings People Look Forward To

By Club.House Editorial · Updated October 30, 2026 · Related guides

Agendas, facilitation, timing and follow-up habits that make meetings worth attending.

A great meeting leaves people wanting the next one. A bad one makes them quietly check their calendars. The difference is rarely charisma — it's structure.

Before the meeting

  • Send the agenda 48 hours ahead so people come prepared.
  • Keep business to under a quarter of the time. People join clubs for the activity, not the admin.
  • Assign a timekeeper and a note-taker.
  • Prepare the room — chairs in a circle for discussion, name tags for newcomers.

A proven agenda shape

  1. Welcome and introductions (5–10%)
  2. Quick approval of last minutes (5%)
  3. Reports from officers (10%)
  4. Main activity or discussion (50–60%)
  5. New business and open floor (10%)
  6. Announcements and next date (5%)

Our agenda builder creates this shape automatically with clock times.

During the meeting

  • Start on time. Waiting for latecomers punishes those who came early.
  • Invite quiet voices by name, gently.
  • Park tangents on a visible list to revisit later.
  • Decide, don't drift. End each discussion item with a decision, an owner or a deadline.

After the meeting

  • Send a short recap within 24 hours: decisions, owners, next date, a photo.
  • Thank anyone who contributed.
  • Follow up personally with first-time attendees.

Online and hybrid meetings

Keep cameras optional but encouraged, use the chat for questions, and limit sessions to about an hour. For hybrid meetings, have a dedicated person watching the online participants so they aren't forgotten.
