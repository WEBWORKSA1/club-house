Club Insurance Basics: Do You Need Liability Cover?

By Club.House Editorial · Updated October 30, 2026 · Related guides

When clubs need insurance, the main policy types and questions to ask before you buy.

Many small clubs never need insurance. But as soon as you rent venues, run physical activities, host the public or handle significant money, a single incident can land on organizers personally. Here's how to think about it.

Signs your club should look into cover

  • A venue or council asks for proof of liability insurance.
  • You run physical activities — sports, hiking, dance, workshops with tools.
  • You host public events or serve food and drink.
  • You hold equipment or club property of real value.
  • You have volunteers or officers making financial decisions.

Common policy types

PolicyWhat it generally covers
General / public liabilityClaims from third parties for injury or property damage at club activities
Event insuranceOne-off cover for a specific event, often required by venues
Directors & officers (D&O)Claims against officers over decisions made on the club's behalf
Property / equipmentLoss, theft or damage to club-owned items
Participant accidentMedical or accident costs for members during activities

Questions to ask before you buy

  1. Which activities are covered — and which are excluded?
  2. Are volunteers and officers covered personally?
  3. Is there a per-event limit or an annual aggregate?
  4. Does the policy satisfy your venue's requirements?
  5. Is cover available through a national federation you could join?

Reduce risk regardless

Use simple sign-in sheets, brief participants on safety, keep a first-aid kit, and write down your emergency plan. Many national sports and hobby federations include basic cover in affiliation fees — check before buying separately.
